1. How does the directive define statutory interest for late payment?

   Answer:
   The directive defines "statutory interest for late payment" as simple interest for late payment at a rate equal to the sum of the reference rate and at least eight percentage points. For euro Member States, the reference rate is either the interest rate applied by the European Central Bank to its most recent main refinancing operations or the marginal interest rate resulting from variable-rate tender procedures for its most recent main refinancing operations (with the rate in force on 1 January applying for the first semester, and the rate on 1 July applying for the second semester). For Member States whose currency is not the euro, it is the equivalent rate set by the national central bank.

   Citation: Source 1, Article 2(6), Article 2(7), and Article 3(2).

2. The owner's note says the EU maximum late fee is 25 EUR. Is that right?

   Answer:
   No, that is not right. The directive does not establish a maximum late fee of 25 EUR. Instead, Article 6(1) provides that the creditor is entitled to obtain, as a minimum, a fixed sum of EUR 40 when interest for late payment becomes payable. In addition, Article 6(3) entitles the creditor to obtain reasonable compensation for any recovery costs exceeding that fixed sum incurred due to the debtor's late payment (such as expenses for a lawyer or debt collection agency).

   Citation: Source 1, Article 6(1), Article 6(3); and Source 2, paragraph 1.

3. Does the business have to send a reminder before it is entitled to interest and to
   the fixed compensation sum?

   Answer:
   No. The business is not required to send a reminder. In commercial transactions between undertakings, the creditor is entitled to interest for late payment "without the necessity of a reminder" once the due date passes, provided the creditor has fulfilled its contractual and legal obligations and the debtor is responsible for the delay. Furthermore, the fixed compensation sum of EUR 40 is explicitly payable "without the necessity of a reminder" as compensation for the creditor's own recovery costs.

   Citation: Source 1, Article 3(1) and Article 6(2).
